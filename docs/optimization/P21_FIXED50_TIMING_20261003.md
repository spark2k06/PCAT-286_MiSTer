## New P06/P12 candidate follow-up (2026-10-04)

The [first synthesized OSD speed candidate](P06_OSD_FIT_20261004.md) adds the
reset-applied eight-profile selector and passes its directed functional checks.
It retains the selected common 50 MHz clocks and constraints, with all features
enabled. Its worst setup is -17.001 ns, compared with the selected fixed-50
baseline's -0.022 ns residual; hold remains positive, minimum +0.076 ns.

The new worst CPU cone connects candidate-head decode and live timing metadata
through interval/deadline qualification into functional executor/register/event
control. A second bridge/cache admission cone also violates setup. Preparing
and retaining budgets separately from public permits is the first structural
experiment to evaluate, with MAX, REP, events and late outcomes checked before
another fresh fit. Relaxing constraints or selecting a lower virtual MHz
profile does not close the physical 20 ns paths. This follow-up remains open;
the measured candidate is not a timing-closed release. The dated baseline
milestones below retain their original evidence and scope.

## Registered prefetch head selection: verified experiment

The eager-data/factored-bridge fit exposes a CPU path from binary prefetch
head decoding to the register file. Replace only the three-bit binary head
with a six-bit one-hot head, initialized to byte zero and rotated on the
same `do_consume` edge. The six AND/five OR byte selection remains intact.
Tail, count, all six storage bytes, fill/consume handshakes, capacity and
pipeline clocks/cycles remain unchanged. The source state grows from 58 to
61 bits: a net three FF bits, with no extra queue bytes or memory blocks.
This removes the combinational head decode and binary-head increment; a
physical improvement still requires a fresh complete fit.

`scripts/prove_at_prefetch_onehot.py` guards exactly five intrinsic normalized
source replacements. Its complete transition proof couples the eight
unchanged state groups (tail/count/six bytes) as arbitrary identical state;
the old head is legal 0..5 and the new head equals its one-hot encoding.
Every one of the four original public outputs is compared without masks,
including `consume_data` while empty. All next-state bits are compared or
related; actual positive-edge clock and unchanged FF descriptors are checked.
Separate reset and flush theorems establish the relation while retaining
common arbitrary unreset storage. No occupancy, fill-count or external
protocol assumption is imposed. A real swapped-byte negative fails with
the prior relation true and an immediate public-output mismatch.
The scope begins after sampled reset/flush; it does not claim power-on
head equality before reset or four-state X equivalence.
